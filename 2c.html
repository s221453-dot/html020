Sign-Magnitude 0

Rule: MSB indicates sign (0 = positive, +; 1 = negative, -). Remaining bits store absolute magnitude.

Formula: (-1)^s × magnitude
Two's Complement 0

Rule: MSB has a negative place value (e.g. -8 for 4-bit). Remaining bits are positive weights. Allows seamless addition and single zero!

Formula: -s·2^(n-1) + Σ(b_i·2^i)

The "Zero" Problem

Sign-Magnitude has two representations for zero: 0000 (+0) and 1000 (-0). This complicates arithmetic comparisons.

Two's Complement Solution: Only one unique zero (0000), reclaiming an extra negative number!
⚖️

Representable Ranges (N-bit)

For n bits:

  • Sign-Magnitude: -(2^(n-1)-1) to +(2^(n-1)-1)
  • Two's Complement: -2^(n-1) to +(2^(n-1)-1)
4-bit Range: SM: -7 to +7 | 2's Comp: -8 to +7
⚡

Hardware Simplicity

In Two's Complement, subtraction is performed simply by negating the subtrahend and adding! No complex sign-checking circuitry required for arithmetic logic units (ALUs).

Result: Faster processors, fewer logic gates, standard addition circuits handle both positive and negative numbers!
